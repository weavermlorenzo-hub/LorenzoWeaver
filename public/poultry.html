Poultry

Poultry is the largest agricultural industry in Georgia by a wide margin — bigger than peaches, pecans, peanuts, and cotton combined. Middle Georgia is dotted with long, low chicken houses, most of them under contract to one of the big integrators headquartered a few hours up the road in Gainesville.

How the industry is organized

A poultry farm in Georgia almost never owns the birds. The integrator — Pilgrim's, Wayne-Sanderson, Tyson, Perdue — delivers day-old chicks and the feed, sets the health protocols, and picks the birds up when they reach market weight. The farmer owns the houses, the labor, and the risk of running them. A typical broiler farm has four to eight houses, each holding 25,000 birds, and turns about six flocks a year.

Why here

Poultry moved into Georgia in the 1930s and never left. The climate works, land was cheap, and the state built a feed-mill and processing infrastructure that made it hard for growers anywhere else to compete on price. Middle Georgia specifically benefits from being close to Interstate 75 and the Port of Savannah — the birds move by refrigerated truck to grocery distribution centers as far away as Chicago and by container to buyers overseas.

Poultry facts

State rank
#1 broiler-producing state in the US.
Annual production
About 1.3 billion birds a year.
Farm-gate value
Over $5 billion, roughly half of Georgia's total farm income.
Grow-out time
Six to eight weeks from chick to market weight.
