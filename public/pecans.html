Pecans

Georgia grows more pecans than any other state, and most of them come out of the counties that stretch from Macon south to Albany. A mature pecan orchard is one of the most recognizable landscapes in middle Georgia — hundred-foot trees on a straight grid, casting shade you can drive a tractor under.

A slow crop

Pecans are patient. A grafted tree takes six or seven years to bear its first real crop and doesn't hit full production until year 12 or 15. In return, a healthy tree will keep producing for a century. That timeline shapes how orchards are owned: pecans in this part of the state tend to stay in the same family for generations, because nobody plants an orchard they don't expect their grandchildren to be harvesting.

Harvest

Pecans come in from mid-October through December. Modern orchards use mechanical shakers that grip the trunk and vibrate the nuts loose, then a second machine sweeps them off the ground. A single Stuart or Desirable tree in a good year can drop over 100 pounds of nuts. Prices move with the Chinese export market — a hard freeze in one growing region can double the price for growers in another.

Pecan facts

State production
Around 100 million pounds a year — about a third of the US crop.
Top varieties
Stuart, Desirable, Cape Fear, and the disease-resistant Sumner.
Trees per acre
About 27 on a 40×40 spacing when mature.
Native range
Pecans are the only major nut crop native to North America.
