Peaches

Georgia has been the Peach State on paper since 1995, but middle Georgia has been the peach state in practice since the 1870s, when Samuel Rumph's orchards near Marshallville proved the fruit would ship north by rail without turning to mush.

Who grows them

Peach County, Crawford County, and Macon County are the core of the commercial peach belt. Two families — the Dickeys of Musella and the Pearsons of Fort Valley — grow a large share of what leaves the state. Georgia produces around 130 million pounds of peaches in an average year, which puts it third nationally behind California and South Carolina but ahead of everyone else. The middle-Georgia climate is actually a little too warm for peaches most winters; growers watch chill hours the way row-crop farmers watch rainfall.

When the season runs

The first peaches — early varieties like Flavorich and Gulfking — come off the trees around the middle of May. Peak season is mid-June through July, when the Elbertas and the newer Julyprince crop is picked. The last of the crop, the O'Henrys, are usually done by mid-August. If you're buying at a roadside stand between Macon and Perry in July, whatever's in the crate that morning was on the tree yesterday.

Peach facts

Trees per acre
About 120, on a standard 20-foot spacing.
Yield
Roughly 300 bushels per acre in a healthy orchard.
Chill hours needed
650 to 850 hours below 45°F over the winter.
Life of an orchard
15 to 20 years before replanting.
