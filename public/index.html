Farming in the Heart of Georgia

The red-clay counties between Macon and Milledgeville have grown some version of the same short list of crops for two hundred years — peaches and pecans in the summer heat, peanuts and cotton coming out of the fields in the fall, and chicken houses running all year long.

Why the middle of the state?

Middle Georgia sits on the fall line, the low ridge where the rolling Piedmont meets the flat Coastal Plain. The soils change over just a few miles: sandy loam south of the line grows peaches and pecans well; the heavier clay north of it was the historical cotton country. Rainfall averages 45 to 50 inches a year and the growing season runs about 220 days, which is long enough for two cash crops in the same field.

What you'll find in this site

Four short pages on the crops and animals that still define the region: peaches, pecans, peanuts, and poultry. Each one covers a bit of history, who grows it today, and when it comes in.

Baldwin County
Home to Milledgeville and a working dairy corridor.
Houston County
Pecans, peanuts, and the state's largest fresh peach shipper.
Bibb County
Macon — the market town all of this passed through.
